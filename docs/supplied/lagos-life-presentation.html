What This Means If You Clone It

The good news: the whole game is one JSON blob in a single table, saved with a PUT. You do not need a complex schema, a message queue, or websockets to reproduce it. A Next.js app, one Postgres table for saves, and a polling loop is genuinely enough — that is exactly what the original does.

The catch: because the server trusts the client's blob, there is no server-side authority over the game state. That is the same property that makes balances editable. If the Irish version is going to have any real economy or payments, the money and inventory fields need to be moved server-side and validated, or you inherit the identical problem. Section 9 and the API section cover this in detail.

Game Overview

What It Actually Is

Lagos Life is a browser-based 2D life-sim built around a top-down grid house, a phone UI, and a persistent save that runs in real time. You create a sim, are assigned a birth origin, move into a home in one of three Nigerian cities (Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt), and then live a life: needs drain, you work shifts, train skills, climb a five-tier career, make friends with real players and NPCs, buy cars, run businesses, commit crimes, go to university, marry, have children, and gamble.

It is not a single-player story game. It is a shared persistent world: the homepage shows who is online right now, there is player-to-player money transfer, direct messaging, a social feed, blocking and reporting, and a government/policy system that affects everyone. The tone is affectionate Lagos street humour — the moodlet list (section 19) is the best evidence of that.

The loop is a genuine time-and-money pressure system, not a checklist. Needs decay at fixed rates per hour (hunger 5.5, bladder 7.0 — the fastest). Every fix costs money. Every shift costs hours you could have spent training. The promotions are gated on skill levels 3, 5, 7 and 9. So the whole game is a question of how you allocate a finite day between earning, training, and staying alive — and the origin you were assigned changes the answer completely.

The Government Layer

There is a global policy system the game calls POLICIES. Exactly one policy is active at a time and it affects every player:

🚐 Transport subsidyDanfo and keke fares are half price
💼 Minimum wage raiseEveryone earns 15% more per shift
🎉 Owambe seasonFun at places around Lagos is 30% stronger
🍛 Food subsidyGroceries and food are 30% cheaper

The live homepage was showing “Owambe season is law this week”, so this is an active, cycling system rather than a dead feature. There is also a government NPC account (@Franka) shown publicly alongside civic stats (“Sea Plots”, “400 homes”) — this is the world-state display on the landing page.

Monetisation

The game is free to play and monetises through top-up packs — the packs are themed and named: Jollof Pack, Owambe Pack, Big Boy Pack, Odogwu Pack, spanning roughly ₦50,000 up to ₦1.6M of in-game naira per purchase. There is also an advertising product where players pay to place ads in-game (₦250,000 standard, ₦500,000 for airport placement), and a daily gem hunt that pays a real prize pool (₦50,000 for first place) as a retention hook.

Payments run through Bachs, a Nigerian payment provider.

Character System

Traits — Choose Exactly 2

You pick two traits at character creation. The button reads “Choose 1 more” until you have two, then it unlocks. Each trait is a permanent modifier on your save — it changes how fast a specific skill grows, how fast a need drains, and how much fun certain activities give. The game has ten traits defined; eight are offered in the creation screen.

Called “Dream” in the creation wizard (step 3 of 5). You pick one. It is a live progress tracker shown in the Goals screen, with a progress formula the game evaluates every tick. There are exactly five.

How the Yaba Unicorn funding roll actually works. When you pitch at CcHub, the success chance is 12% + (coding − 5) × 5% + (charisma − 3) × 5% + 10% if netWorth > 40× …. On success, the cheque size is rolled: 10% chance of ₦1,000,000 (“A VC led a ₦1M round! Your startup is the talk of Yaba 🦄🚀”), 20% of ₦500,000, 35% of ₦250,000, otherwise ₦100,000. This is the single biggest legitimate payday in the game and the only way to finish that goal.

Starting Origins — Nepo Baby vs LAPO Baby

This is the mechanic that most shapes a playthrough, and it is decided once per account, by the server, and cannot be changed. The creation wizard calls it the Birth lottery (step 4 of 5): you drag to spin, the server returns your origin, and the screen says plainly: “Decided once for your account. Starting a new life won’t change it.” Starting a new sim keeps the same origin forever.

Technically: the client calls GET /api/origin and the server answers {"origin":"nepo","fresh":false}. The fresh flag marks whether this is the first time the account has been assigned one. The origin is then written into the save by applyOrigin(game, origin).

Daddy’s Rent Deal — The Hidden Cost

Nepo rent is free until you call in a favour. The moment you use “Call Daddy” to pay a bail, the game sets daddyDebt to the bail amount and stops paying your rent. From then on:

Call Daddy for bail
→
Daddy pays the bail
→
Daddy stops paying rent
→
You pay full rent each week
→
Each payment reduces daddyDebt
→
Debt cleared
→
Daddy pays rent again 💅

There is also a lock-out: daddyBailLock() returns “📵 Daddy isn’t picking your calls this week” if you call him again too soon. The whole system is a nice piece of design — the Nepo start is powerful but has a debt mechanic that bites exactly when you are already in trouble.

An important trap I hit while verifying. A fresh Nepo account showed ₦1,500,000 in the wallet, not ₦500,000. That is not the origin value — it is a temporary promotional gift. The game carries a hard-coded gift event:

{
  id: "gift-2026-10-04",
  amount: 1_000_000,
  until: Date.UTC(2026, 9, 11, 23, 0),   // 11 Oct 2026
  text: "A gift from the Lagos Life team 🎁 ₦1,000,000 is in your wallet."
}

So ₦500,000 (Nepo) + ₦1,000,000 (team gift) = ₦1,500,000. The base Nepo starting money is ₦500,000. If you clone this game, do not copy the promotional gift into the base economy — and if you clone it with the gift, remember to expire it, because it is dated.

How Skill Growth Actually Works

Base rateEach practice action adds a fixed fractional amount per action or per hour.
Trait multiplierIf a chosen trait names that skill, the gain is multiplied — 1.5× or 1.6× depending on the trait.
LAPO originA flat +25% to every skill, on top of any trait multiplier. This is the strongest single learning bonus in the game.
Action multiplierSome actions are simply better than others: Filming a Comedy Skit (1.5×) beats Practising Stand-up (1.3×).
Level cap10 for every skill. Skills are not consumed — levelling never costs money.

Why this matters for the Ireland port. The skill list is the spine of the whole career system: each of the 30 careers names exactly one skill, and most careers are gated behind that skill reaching level 2 or 3. Get the nine skills and their multipliers right and the rest of the economy falls out of it. Do not carry over generic Sims-style skills (Logic, Handiness, Creativity) — this game does not have them.

Abuja & Port Harcourt

Each city has its own location set, its own homes, its own careers and its own NPCs. The cities are not cosmetic reskins — they have different economies (Abuja is civil-service and market trade, PH is oil, ferries and bole), different pay ceilings, and different moods. Abuja’s locations include the Federal Secretariat, Wuse Market, Gwagwalada motor park, Garki Police Division and an EFCC office. PH’s include Creek Road, the Bonny jetty, Mama Ibiba’s Bole Spot, PH Mall and the PH Oil Base.

Each location is a fully modelled 3D-ish scene, not a menu. Behind every location there is a scene definition with floor and wall colours, a sign with its own text and colours, lighting mood, stations (each with an emoji, a position and its own action list), decor props, and a crowd of NPCs with individual positions, facing angles, poses and role descriptions.

For example, the Garki Police Division has a front desk where a “Desk sergeant writing in the big book” works, a cell, a waiting bench, a poster reading “POLICE IS YOUR FRIEND”, a notice board reading “FCT POLICE COMMAND · GARKI”, and ambience lines including “Oga, na misunderstanding!” and “Statements are taken one by one”. The EFCC office similarly has an operative lounge, a “SAY NO TO CORRUPTION” poster, and a crowd including a “Lawyer with a thick file” and someone “Invited for questioning, sweating”.

This is a significant amount of authored content per location, and it is the main reason the game feels alive rather than like a spreadsheet. It is also the biggest content cost in any clone — budget for it.

Getting Around Lagos — The Real Cost Model

Fares are not flat. Each mode has a base fare, a per-kilometre rate, a minutes-per-km speed, and a traffic sensitivity multiplier. The traffic number is what makes this interesting: danfo and cab have traffic sensitivity 1.0 (fully exposed to go-slow), while okada is only 0.35 — which is exactly why okada is worth the danger in Lagos.

Worked example. A 10 km trip: Trek costs nothing but takes 120 minutes. Danfo costs ₦100 + ₦50 = ₦150 in 30 minutes. Okada costs ₦150 + ₦80 = ₦230 in 20 minutes. Cab costs ₦300 + ₦150 = ₦450. Exam grading is a real scale. A score of 70+ is an A (5 grade points), 60+ a B (4), 50+ a C (3), 45+ a D (2), 40+ an E (1), and below 40 an F (0). CGPA is points ÷ units, and the degree class is derived from it: 4.5+ is First Class, 3.5+ is 2:1, 2.4+ is 2:2, below that is a Third.

Your degree class gives a permanent percentage bonus to job pay, for the rest of the save. This is the single biggest reason to go to university.

Combined with the +5% same-field bonus, a First Class Law graduate who becomes a lawyer is earning roughly +25% over an unqualified hire at the same level, before any trait or blessing multipliers.

ASUU Strikes — The Authentic Touch

The game models ASUU strikes (the Academic Staff Union of Universities). After you have been enrolled for more than 10,080 minutes (7 days), there is a 4% chance per day that a strike is declared. It lasts 2 to 4 days, and while it is on:

ClassesBlocked — “ASUU is on strike 😩 No classes till it’s called off”
ExamsPostponed — “ASUU is on strike 😩 Exams are postponed”
LibraryStays open — you can still study
Moodlet“Strike Blues” — a negative moodlet

When it ends: “ASUU has called off the strike 🎉 Lectures resume: back to the Lecture Theatre!” Modelling a real, painful, well-known Nigerian institution as a random 4%-per-day event that blocks your progress is exactly the kind of specificity that makes this game feel local rather than generic.

Life After Graduation

Graduation changes your status in specific ways: you cannot stay in the halls (“Graduates can’t stay in the halls 🎓”) and are moved out into a Mushin room after 7 days — “The hall porter says graduates must pack out 📦 You moved into a room in Mushin with all your things.” You keep alumni access to the library and the buka. It is a small but very effective piece of design: graduation has a real, slightly sad cost.

Power is a real system with three states and three solutions. NEPA (the national grid) cuts out at random intervals set at game creation (powerChangeAt = now + 5–12 hours). When it cuts, anything with a power requirement stops working — unless you have a generator or solar.

🛂 Visa System & "Japa" — Travel to Ireland

The core design pillar: Ireland is not a city you unlock — it's a country you earn. Two paths exist: the legal visa route (expensive, slow, uncertain) and "japa" (illegal border crossing — cheaper, faster, but with severe consequences). Both are fully integrated into the existing travel, economy, and social systems.

Design Philosophy

The visa system is not a single check — it's a multi-stage gauntlet that mirrors real Nigerian experiences with Schengen/UK visa applications. Every stage costs money, time, or both. Rejection is common and not the end — you can reapply, but each attempt leaves a record. The "japa" path exists as a parallel track: no paperwork, no fees, but you arrive with nothing, no legal status, and constant risk of detection. The tension between the two paths is the gameplay.

Economy Integration

Visa costs are a significant money sink — a full tourist visa application costs ~₦150,000–₦200,000 in documents and fees. Work permits cost ~₦500,000+. This creates a natural progression:

Earn in Lagos → Save for visa → Apply → Wait → (maybe rejected) → Reapply or Japa → Arrive in Ireland → Work → Earn in € → Send money home

The /api/send money transfer system already exists — it can be extended for international transfers with a 5% FX fee and 1–3 day processing time.
