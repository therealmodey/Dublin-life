# Ireland Life product review

Reviewed in full: `/Users/therealmodey/ireland-life-prd-v2.md` (966 lines) and `/Users/therealmodey/lagos-life-presentation.html` (1,991 lines; extracted its visible text and inspected its structural/code-bearing sections as text). This is an assessment of supplied documents only. The HTML and PRD are claims and proposals, not independently verified evidence about the live service.

## What the documents describe

The target is **Ireland Life**, a Lagos Life extension: a player begins in Lagos, builds resources, and tries to reach and establish a life in Ireland through a legal visa path or an undocumented “japa” path. The PRD's central loop is Lagos livelihood → save and prepare → visa application and wait → approval/rejection → travel or japa → survive in Dublin → thrive/remit (PRD §§1, 4, lines 31–310). The loop also carries ordinary life-sim play: work, needs, housing, transport, social ties and cultural activities.

The proposed world is Dublin-only at launch, with Lagos as the starting city; Cork/Galway and other expansions are deferred (PRD §§5, 13, lines 312–379 and 913–927). The PRD specifies four visa types, five irregular routes, undocumented penalties and four regularisation routes (PRD §§6–7, lines 380–641); 25 Dublin locations, 10 careers and 15 moodlets (PRD §8, lines 643–726); and cross-city chat without visits (F13, lines 316–330). Its MVP also calls for a full Lagos Life clone, housing, transport, exchange and international flights.

The presentation supplies a detailed *description* of Lagos Life's alleged systems—needs, skills, jobs, homes, social/economy, locations, UI flows, API shapes, and a database sketch (HTML §§1–23, lines 128–1496)—then proposes Ireland adaptations (§24, lines 1497–1748) and visa/japa additions (lines 1749–1990). It is useful as a research and design reference, especially for identifying candidate systems and content volume.

## What is supported, proposed, or inferred

- **Documented as claims:** The presentation says it inspected shipped bundles, public API/pages and two test accounts (HTML subtitle and “Verified against…” labels, lines 83–91; closing claim, 1987). Its descriptions of the current Lagos implementation, exact balances, endpoint behavior and schema remain author-reported; these files do not include inspection artifacts, source code, API captures or reproducible evidence.
- **Proposed design:** The Ireland city/career/location/moodlet mappings, new visa endpoints and fields, and implementation order are recommendations, not existing functionality (HTML §24, lines 1497–1748; visa technical proposal, 1913–1952; PRD draft status, lines 1–10).
- **Not present:** The HTML is a static documentation/presentation artifact. It has headings, tables, prose and inline code examples, but no `<script>` blocks, app implementation, bundled source, database, assets, or executable game logic. The PRD is prose/tables/diagrams. Neither document provides a buildable Lagos Life codebase.
- **Inference:** The documents are enough to scope and prototype an original Ireland Life slice, but not enough to recreate Lagos Life faithfully or implement the complete proposed game without product, balance, content and technical decisions.

## Product and specification gaps

1. **Conflicting scope.** PRD says Dublin only and frames Ireland as an earned country (lines 33–43, 913–927), while the adaptation plan says preserve three cities and describes sets for Cork and Galway (HTML lines 1502–1508, 1569 onward). Even within the presentation, the adaptation order says “one city (Dublin)” (line 1745), but its three-city recommendation is unresolved. Choose the actual launch world and define whether a Lagos Life account/world is reused or this is a new game.
2. **Passport arithmetic and timing conflict.** PRD §6.2 totals passport acquisition at ₦80,000 and about five weeks, including birth and state-of-origin documents (lines 393–404). The presentation's visa flow lists a ₦50,000 passport taking two weeks (HTML lines 1767–1780), and its adaptation prose elsewhere also gives ₦50,000/two weeks. Decide the canonical quest, costs and time. A player-facing “visa” also mixes a Nigerian passport quest, biometrics in Lagos, Irish visa categories and a separate Irish Embassy location; the location/authority ownership needs a coherent flow.
3. **Visa rules are placeholders rather than a model.** Approval starts at differing base rates (PRD lines 382–390), then the modifier table stacks multiple bonuses and penalties without specifying ordering, caps, randomness, repeatability or visa-specific eligibility (lines 462–478). Visa fee/processing/stay/work-rights tables and the decision outcome table use inconsistent levels of detail (lines 382–390, 420–455). “Investor / Golden Visa” is named without a distinct gameplay loop. Define a deterministic balance model and the minimum supported visa paths before implementation.
4. **Time semantics and pacing are undefined.** The PRD uses days, weeks, months and real-time checking (lines 220–309, 393–455), while the described base game has accelerated simulation time and polling (HTML lines 205–215). It never says what is real time versus simulated time, how offline progress works, or how a 15–90-day wait remains engaging. This choice affects persistence, retention, UI and backend scheduling.
5. **Japa route design is mechanically precise but product validation is thin.** Five real-world dangerous routes are assigned prices, success percentages, travel durations and severe outcomes (PRD lines 524–600; HTML lines 1827–1866). The numbers have no source or balancing method, and route outcomes do not fully align: e.g., routes arrive first in Italy/Spain/Greece/UK but route to Ireland is underspecified. The design needs an agreed tone/representation review and coherent arrival rules before content is built. The present specification should not be treated as factual guidance about immigration.
6. **Economy and monetization cannot yet be balanced.** The PRD says retain a free-to-play model and use EUR top-ups/Stripe (lines 787–821), while the presentation recommends rescaling wages but retaining ratios and gives conflicting Euro property examples (HTML lines 1510–1518, 1556–1567). There is no single currency conversion/economy sheet, legal/product decision on paid currency, pricing, refunds, or a server-authoritative transaction design. The presentation itself warns that the described Lagos client-save model trusts a client blob and permits editable balances (HTML lines 160–176); it also sketches Stripe migration without implementation details.
7. **Social safety and operations are missing.** Cross-city chat is P0, while the PRD does not define chat controls, abuse reporting/review, account moderation, privacy/retention, age policy or escalation. The presentation lists block/report endpoints and tables (HTML lines 1356–1414, 1466–1480), but no moderation workflow or admin tooling. This is a launch blocker for a persistent social game, especially since the secondary audience includes ages 16–17 (PRD lines 79–87). The proposed remake must specify these systems rather than assume the scrape's brief API/schema sketch is sufficient.
8. **MVP is too large to estimate from the roadmap.** “Full Lagos Life clone with all systems” plus immigration, cross-city social, housing and Ireland content is P0 (PRD lines 312–330). The presentation says the adaptation entails 30 career ladders/150 titles, 133 moodlets, 28 modeled locations, 14 floor plans, businesses and dialogue (HTML lines 1743–1745); PRD schedules the broader build in 14 weeks (lines 823–895) without team size, reuse assumptions, acceptance criteria, content pipeline or QA scope. The roadmap is a rough aspiration, not a defensible estimate.

## Minimum playable scope I recommend

Treat this as a new game slice, not a faithful clone. Build one player save, one Lagos starting scenario and one Dublin destination; a minimal job/savings loop; one legal tourist path with passport/document checklist, application, wait/decision, and flight; a clear Dublin arrival state with a few authored locations, one job and housing choice; basic needs/actions; and a small set of meaningful social/emotional events. Defer other visa types, all japa routes, regularisation, payment/top-ups, other cities, full 30-career parity, casinos/businesses and broad social launch until the core loop is playable and reviewed. Keep the first slice single-player or closed test until safety/moderation and economy authority are specified.

## Build blockers versus ordinary choices

**Blockers for a faithful clone or public social launch:** the actual Lagos Life source/repository and reusable assets are absent; the supplied HTML is not source. There is no verified live evidence bundle to substantiate implementation claims. The money/save authority and payment design are unresolved. The visa/japa loop has contradictions and unspecified time semantics. Public chat lacks an operational safety/moderation policy and tools. Irish-specific dialogue/content has not been approved by local reviewers.

**Routine product/engineering decisions once those blockers are addressed:** exact Dublin venue selection, final job titles and wages, UI presentation, initial moodlet names, and which small set of non-core activities enters the first prototype. These can be iterated from a chosen playable slice rather than solved across all 30 careers up front.
