# Dublin roads and port evidence

This note supports the standalone explorer's authored streetscape and port interpretation. World coordinates, lengths, widths, and timing are compressed scene units and simulation seconds. They are not surveyed geometry, live port operating data, a ferry timetable, nor evidence about private Lagos Life systems.

## Port infrastructure

- Dublin Port Company's [Alexandra Basin Redevelopment](https://www.dublinport.ie/masterplan/abr-project/) documents quay-wall construction, basin/channel dredging and Ro-Ro infrastructure. The model uses a quay wall, coping, berth edge and linkspan/ramp to communicate those visible port functions.
- The Port Company's [Alexandra Quay Container Terminal opening](https://www.dublinport.ie/dublin-port-company-opens-new-alexandra-quay-container-terminal/) states that the terminal has RTG crane runways and ship-to-shore gantry cranes. This supports the terminal stacks, yard crane and ship-to-shore crane silhouettes; their quantity, sizes and placement in this scene are illustrative.
- Dublin Port's [Masterplan 2040 and estate maps](https://www.dublinport.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Infrastructure-Charges-2026-to-2030_appendices_final-for-publication_28-11-28.pdf) label Lo-Lo container yards and terminals, Ro-Ro yards, terminal areas, berths, road network and upgraded footways. It supports the distinct cargo yard, Ro-Ro zone and internal access road. It is a planning/estate drawing rather than a survey of this scene.
- The Port Company's [ABR2 consultation](https://www.dublinport.ie/dublin-port-company-launches-public-consultation-on-alexandra-basin-redevelopment-2-abr2/) describes the permitted Unified Ferry Terminal at Terminal 5 and work around Alexandra Basin West. The modeled passenger building is a generic ferry-terminal form, not a claim that its footprint reproduces Terminal 5.
- Dublin Port's [To & From page](https://www.dublinport.ie/to-from/) identifies Irish Ferries at Terminal 1 and the port's passenger/cargo connections. Its 2026 [Notices to Mariners](https://www.dublinport.ie/information-centre/notice-to-mariners-2026/) include compulsory pilotage, pilotage services, pilot transfer, towage, ferry slot times and North Bank Lighthouse. These support the pilot/towage and navigation context. No live schedule or service connection is implemented.
- Dublin Port's [3FM project overview](https://www.dublinport.ie/dublin-port-opens-public-consultation-on-3fm-project/) describes Poolbeg Peninsula port lands, a proposed new container terminal, port park and active-travel routes. The scene's basin, breakwaters, beacon and lighthouse read as an eastward harbor setting; it does not reproduce the future 3FM plan or assert its planning status as built infrastructure.

The port's vessel cycle is a self-contained visual simulation: approach from the outer basin, occupy one of two authored berths, show a crane transfer phase, depart and wait offshore. The pause, resume, dispatch and reset controls affect only this in-memory scene. Counters are generated by that simulation and do not represent Dublin Port throughput.

## Roads and streetscape

- The project's `src/dublin-road-network.js` is the geometric source of truth for city traffic. `src/dublin-road-details.js` follows its sampled lane paths to place footway/kerb ribbons, lane markings and road fittings. This alignment is an implementation fact, not a claim that those routes reproduce Dublin's surveyed street centerlines.
- Dublin City Council's [Clontarf to City Centre Active Travel Scheme](https://www.dublincity.ie/residential/travel-and-transport/active-travel/active-travel-improvement-works/central-area/new-clontarf-city-centre-cycling-and-bus-upgrades) documents bus-priority infrastructure, upgraded walking routes and cycle lanes. The [Barrow Street Public Realm Scheme](https://www.dublincity.ie/public-realm-and-road-maintenance/our-public-realm-plans-and-projects/barrow-street-public-realm-scheme) shows the mix of resurfaced roadway, footpaths, crossings, streetlights, planting, cycle parking and bus/taxi functions used as streetscape cues.
- A Dublin City Council [active-travel general arrangement drawing](https://www.dublincity.ie/sites/default/files/2024-06/general-arrangement_0.pdf) labels bus lanes, cycle tracks, footpaths, pedestrian/toucan crossings, tactile paving and Luas stops. This supports those categories as Dublin infrastructure. The explorer does not claim that all treatments co-occur on every street or accurately depicts accessible tactile details.

The authored terminal aprons occupy x=39..62.5 on the north and south banks (z=-15..-3 and z=3..15), leaving a six-unit-wide open Liffey mouth between them. A pedestrian/service bridge at x≈42.4 has sloped approaches and exposes its changing walking elevation through `groundY`; only its deck and ramps count as land within the river channel. A supported access embankment joins the eastbound city edge at z≈31 to the port gate near x=42, z≈15. This is a compressed visual connection, not a surveyed road alignment. The port spur is static streetscape geometry and is not added to `DUBLIN_ROAD_LANES`; no moving traffic is simulated on it.

Road-detail verification samples fitting placements against the Liffey crossing rules, confirms every mesh has a Three.js material, and checks that tram-rail fittings stay clear of shared traffic-lane samples. Port checks compute mesh bounds for container-stack and gantry-leg support, test that fixed quay equipment does not occupy the navigable mouth, and sample the vessel cycle for quay incursions, vessel overlaps, and breakwater/beacon collisions. These checks validate authored scene geometry and local animation behavior only.
