# Dublin Airport expansion notes

The airport lot is placed at `(-11, -55)` and its ground footprint is 72 by 29 scene units. The airport factory keeps its compact local authoring coordinates and scales only the campus group horizontally; parked aircraft and the maintenance helicopter use inverse horizontal scales to keep their source proportions. Both parallel runway surfaces render 64 units long, the crosswind runway renders 22 units long, and all three surfaces fit inside the campus. The runway thresholds and both gate/taxi routes use the expanded world coordinates, so aircraft do not use stale pre-expansion positions.

## Evidence used

- Dublin Airport's [Runway Operations](https://www.dublinairport.com/corporate/environmental-social-governance/noise/runway-and-flight-paths/runway-operations) page identifies the South Runway 10R/28L, North Runway 10L/28R, and crosswind runway 16/34. Those designations and axes are retained in the scene. The model does not represent the airport's operational runway-selection rules.
- Dublin Airport's [interactive terminal map](https://www.dublinairport.com/at-the-airport/the-map?map=true) and its [transport connections map](https://www.dublinairport.com/docs/default-source/bus-timetables/dap_transport_map_website_1092-x-1382.pdf?sfvrsn=53a857c8_2) establish separate T1 and T2 facilities, car parks, taxi stands, bus/coach access, and terminal approach areas. Those are the basis for the two terminal fronts, taxi/coach bays, access lanes, and small parking area in this compressed scene.
- The saved public Lagos Life bundle at `reference/public-source/city-map-reference.js` (airport scene code around character offsets 47,700–55,000) was inspected as a visual-complexity reference. It contains a layered airport campus with terminal signage, gates, service/landside pieces and repeated detail. Dublin uses its own researched terminal/runway arrangement and does not claim to reproduce Lagos Life's private implementation.

## Scene detail

The expanded layout contains T1/T2 halls and concourses, an arched mesh barrel canopy on T2, twelve boarding-bridge detail pieces across four stands, two active scheduled gates, three parked airliners, terminal forecourt and access roads, taxi and coach bays, staff parking, two maintenance hangars, a fuel compound, baggage staging/tugs, perimeter fencing and apron floodlights. A planted circular island sits inside a ring-shaped access road. Repeated markings and equipment use two instanced batches. Static buildings, service vehicles, equipment, raised details, parked aircraft, the parked helicopter and the T2 canopy all fit within the campus bounds.

The focused clearance test samples both scheduled aircraft every 0.1 seconds over one 120-second cycle against 278 raised airport obstacle bounds, for 667,200 aircraft-obstacle checks. It records zero forbidden intersections. A small boarding-door shoe intersection remains intentional and is capped at 0.01 cubic scene units; the airport detail inventory and clearance result are available in `airport.userData.metrics` during the audit.

The airport dimensions and all object spacing are scene units for the visual explorer, not surveyed measurements. The simplified runway depiction and scheduled traffic are illustrative. Fuel tanks, service vehicles, hangars, parking, fence and apron equipment provide recognizable airport context; their positions, quantities, operating status, and real-world counterparts have not been independently mapped. This remains a standalone map scene, not an integration with Dublin Airport systems or Lagos Life gameplay.
