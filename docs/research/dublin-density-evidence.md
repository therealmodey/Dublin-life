# Dublin infill, parks and landmark form evidence

The map remains a deliberately compressed, low-poly scene in authored world units. The new buildings, roads and landscape details are scene interpretations, not surveyed parcel geometry or claims about the original Lagos Life map. Coordinates describe this explorer only: west is negative X, north is negative Z, and the Liffey crosses the center of the scene. The airport receives a dedicated 72 by 29 unit site north of the city; the runway, terminal and activity are still compact representations.

## Street and residential structure

Residential infill is generated in city blocks spanning the west heritage districts, north inner city, airport edge, south centre and docklands. Every terrace candidate is checked against selectable venue parcels, earlier homes, road centerlines and the walkable land function. Offset, closely spaced rows form denser blocks; small width, garden, roof and door changes retain a coherent Georgian terrace language. Housing may repeat. Public-facing buildings use both site-specific massing and differentiated facades. Major silhouettes include stadiums, churches, the Custom House, Heuston and Connolly Stations, the Wood Quay Civic Offices, Government Buildings, the Mansion House, Central Bank Dockland Campus, named retailers and Poolbeg Lighthouse.

The block extents follow the city and regeneration areas shown on Dublin City Council [Development Plan Map K](https://www.dublincity.ie/sites/default/files/2022-07/map-k.pdf) and its [city map](https://assets-eu-01.kc-usercontent.com/aa24ba70-9a12-01ae-259b-7ef588a0b2ef/25ae3768-e605-4166-a2b6-ae8e11f09c6e/Dublin%20City%20Map%200923-ONLINE-2.pdf). Those sources establish broad urban relationships, not exact home locations or the map's compressed dimensions.

## Park landscape plans

The nine park/green parcels use separate, source-informed compositions with real landscape features built from batched scene geometry. Phoenix Park now combines dense deciduous woodland groups and open paths with low-poly interpretations of the Wellington Testimonial, Papal Cross, Ashtown Castle and a small fallow-deer group. St Stephen’s Green has an ornamental lake, footbridge, bandstand, formal planting and gated walks. Merrion Square adds its perimeter railings, Oscar Wilde memorial, sculpture trail, flower beds and a compact playground. The Garden of Remembrance retains its cruciform reflecting pool and replaces the generic cross marker with the Children of Lir swan memorial. Iveagh Gardens has a sunken panel, clipped yew maze, grotto, rockwork and stepped cascade; the War Memorial Gardens add rose parterres, paired bookrooms and a ceremonial axis. The smaller nature park, neighbourhood green and cathedral-side park include wetland planting, path furniture, bins, cycle stands, railings, lamps and beds. Park-specific plans currently generate 168 tree placements using the established small/large deciduous asset choices.

Dublin City Council’s [flagship park inventory and strategy](https://www.dublincity.ie/dublin-city-parks-strategy/4-resources-and-services/41-parks/412-flagship-parks) distinguishes historic parks such as Phoenix Park, St Stephen’s Green, Merrion Square and the War Memorial Gardens from the contemporary Garden of Remembrance. The official [Phoenix Park guide](https://www.phoenixpark.ie/what-to-see/) identifies the Wellington Testimonial, Papal Cross, Ashtown Castle and fallow deer; the [OPW St Stephen’s Green page](https://heritageireland.ie/places-to-visit/st-stephens-green/) describes its ornamental lake. The Council’s [Merrion Square inventory](https://www.dublincity.ie/dublin-city-parks-strategy/4-resources-and-services/41-parks/412-flagship-parks) documents the sculpture trail, Oscar Wilde memorial, playground and thick perimeter planting. The same Council source explains the Children of Lir swan sculpture and cruciform pool at the [Garden of Remembrance](https://www.dublincity.ie/dublin-city-parks-strategy/4-resources-and-services/41-parks/412-flagship-parks). The [OPW description of Iveagh Gardens](https://heritageireland.ie/visit/places-to-visit/the-iveagh-gardens/) names its grottos, sunken lawns, fountain centres, woodland, maze, rose garden and rockeries; this scene compresses a subset of those features. The War Memorial garden form follows the Council’s park strategy and its [garden description](https://www.dublincity.ie/dublin-city-parks-strategy/4-resources-and-services/41-parks/412-flagship-parks). The Council identifies St Patrick’s Park as adjacent to [St Patrick’s Cathedral](https://www.dublincity.ie/dublin-city-parks-strategy/4-resources-and-services/41-parks/412-flagship-parks); the [Ringsend and Irishtown trail guide](https://www.dublincity.ie/sites/default/files/2022-06/ringsend-irishtown-walking-trail-map-guide-2022.pdf) describes the nature park’s walking trails and coastal setting.

These sources establish identity and distinguishing features, not the compressed dimensions. Sculpture, paths, planting and furniture are low-poly interpretations fitted to the existing selectable parcels. Automated tests count generated geometry and placements; a browser view remains necessary to judge composition and legibility.

## Distinctive structures

Place identity and broad location remain linked to the per-place sources in `src/dublin-locations.js` and the broader evidence in [Dublin city scene evidence](dublin-city-evidence.md). Added structures have primary or official references: the [Wood Quay Civic Offices](https://www.dublincity.ie/sites/default/files/2026-02/art-in-parks-2nd-edition-2025.pdf) are described as a varied-height cluster with an open-air amphitheatre and Viking-inspired entrance sculpture; the [Mansion House](https://www.dublincity.ie/archaeology-conservation-and-heritage/dublins-historic-buildings/mansion-house) has a distinctive Round Room; [Government Buildings](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-the-taoiseach/organisation-information/history-of-government-buildings/) form an Edwardian state complex; the [Central Bank's Dockland Campus](https://www.centralbank.ie/about/our-dockland-campus) comprises two buildings joined by an elevated bridge; and [Heuston Station](https://www.irishrail.ie/en-ie/station/dublin-heuston) is the western intercity terminal. The [National Library](https://www.nli.ie/visit) is on Kildare Street, and the [Poolbeg peninsula walking trail](https://www.dublincity.ie/sites/default/files/2022-06/ringsend-irishtown-walking-trail-map-guide-2022.pdf) identifies the distinctive red lighthouse at the Great South Wall. Pub, station and retail identities are linked to their own sources on each place record.

Those references establish identity and distinguishing features only. The geometry is authored manually, and roofs, elevations, street furniture, exact footprints and façade detailing have not been measured. The facade variants are visual differentiation within the scene, not assertions that those additions appear on the real buildings.

## Verification measurements

`tests/dublin-map.test.mjs` measures the generated home count and footprint collisions, verifies every park gets a named landscape plan, checks landmark silhouettes and compares each non-residential site's actual instanced geometry transforms after removing city position and color. These checks validate authored data and geometry metadata; a browser view remains necessary to judge visual balance and legibility.
